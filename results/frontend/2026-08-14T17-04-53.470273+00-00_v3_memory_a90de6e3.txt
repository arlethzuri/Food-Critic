Based on the Utah Food Service Sanitation Rule (R392-100) and Salt Lake County inspection standards, violations are categorized by their potential impact on public health. While the specific point-deduction formula for the `inspection_score` is not explicitly detailed in the regulatory text provided, the distinction between violation types is defined by the urgency of required corrective action:

### **Critical Violations (Priority Items)**
These are often referred to as "Priority" or "Priority Foundation" items. They represent the highest risk to food safety (e.g., improper temperature control, cross-contamination).
*   **Risk Level:** High; these are direct threats to consumer health.
*   **Correction Requirement:** Must be corrected extremely quickly—often **within 72 hours** of the inspection.
*   **Impact:** Because they pose an immediate hazard, a high number of critical violations is the primary driver of a poor (higher) inspection score and can lead to more frequent follow-up inspections or even temporary closure if not addressed.

### **Non-Critical Violations (Core Items)**
These are categorized as "Core Item" violations. They relate to general sanitation and maintenance but are less likely to cause immediate foodborne illness.
*   **Risk Level:** Lower; these involve long-term sanitation practices or facility maintenance (e.g., cracked floor tiles, minor labeling issues).
*   **Correction Requirement:** A longer timeframe is permitted for correction, typically up to **10 calendar days**.
*   **Impact:** While they contribute to the overall inspection score, they do not carry the same immediate regulatory urgency as priority items.

### **Summary Comparison**

| Feature | Critical (Priority) Violations | Non-Critical (Core) Violations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Risk** | Immediate foodborne illness threat | Long-term sanitation/maintenance issues |
| **Examples** | Temperature abuse, poor hand hygiene | Dirty surfaces, facility maintenance |
| **Correction Window** | Very short (e.g., 72 hours) | Longer (e.g., 10 days) |
| **Score Impact** | High; primary driver of high scores | Moderate; contributes to total score |

***Note:** Most establishments in Salt Lake County (98.4%) do not have matched SLCHD inspection history available in this dataset, so these definitions apply to the regulatory framework used by inspectors during their visits.*
