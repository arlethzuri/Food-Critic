In Salt Lake County, food safety inspections categorize violations based on their potential risk to public health. While the specific numerical point deductions aren't detailed in the provided regulatory snippets, the distinction lies in the **urgency of correction** and the **nature of the hazard**.

### Critical vs. Non-Critical Violations

The regulations distinguish between three main types of items:

*   **Priority Items (Critical):** These are violations that pose the most immediate risk to food safety. Because of the high potential for hazard, they often require correction **at the time of inspection** or within a very short window (up as little as **72 hours**) (R392-100 § 8-405.11).
*   **Priority Foundation Items:** These are also considered higher priority than core items but may have a slightly longer correction window—up to **10 calendar days** after the inspection (R392-100 § 8-405.11).
*   **Core Item Violations (Non-Critical):** These are "core" maintenance or cleanliness issues that, while important for overall sanitation, do not pose the same immediate, high-level risk as priority items.

### Impact on Inspection Score

While the regulation text provided focuses on the **timeframes for correction**, the inspection score is a reflection of these findings. 
*   **Critical violations** (Priority Items) are much more likely to significantly impact an establishment's score because they represent immediate health hazards.
*   The regulatory authority uses these classifications to determine how quickly a restaurant must act to remain in compliance and avoid further enforcement action.

***Note:** Most establishments in the database have no matched SLCHD inspection history, meaning their specific scoring math for individual violations is not publicly visible in this dataset.*
