--- Answer ---
An inspection score is a numerical value assigned during a health inspection that reflects how well a food establishment is complying with the Utah Food Code (R3 $\text{92-100}$). 

While the specific mathematical formula (e.g., starting from 100 and subtracting points) is not explicitly detailed in the provided regulatory text, the score is fundamentally tied to the presence of different types of violations:

*   **Priority Items:** These are violations that pose a significant risk to public health (e.g., improper temperature control).
*   **Priority Foundation Items:** These are items that support the prevention of priority violations (e.g., lack of handwashing sinks).
*   **Core Items:** These are less critical but still important for overall food safety and sanitation (e.g., cleanliness of surfaces).

### Key Takeaways from the Inspection Process:
*   **Lower is Better:** In this dataset, a **lower inspection score indicates a cleaner record**. A score of `0` represents an inspection with no violations found.
*   **Criticality Matters:** The presence of "Priority" or "Critical" violations (often marked with asterisks in the source data) typically has a more significant impact on the establishment's standing and may require faster corrective action.
*   **Corrective Action:** For many priority items, establishments are often given a specific window (such as **10 calendar days**) to correct the violation and have it verified by the regulatory authority.

***Note:** Most restaurants in Salt Lake County (98.4%) do not have a matched SLCHD inspection history available in this dataset.*
