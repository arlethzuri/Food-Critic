In the context of food safety regulations (such as Utah's R392-100), **cold holding** refers to the practice of maintaining **Time/Temperature Control for Safety (TCS) foods** at specific, safe temperatures to prevent the growth of harmful bacteria.

While the regulation does not provide a single dictionary definition for the phrase "cold holding," it places it within the critical framework of temperature management:

*   **Temperature Control:** The regulations emphasize that certain food preparation processes—including **"hot or cold holding"**—are part of managing foods that require strict temperature control (Section 8-302.13).
*   **Preventing Bacterial Growth:** The primary goal of cold holding is to ensure that TCS foods are kept at temperatures specified in the food code (often 41°F/5°C or below) to inhibit the growth of pathogens that could cause foodborne illness.
*   **Equipment Integrity:** Regulations also address equipment failure, noting that if a machine cannot maintain the required food temperatures (as specified under Chapter 3), it poses a risk to food safety (Section 4-204.13).

In short, "cold holding" is the regulatory requirement to keep potentially hazardous foods cold enough to remain safe for consumption.
