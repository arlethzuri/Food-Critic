## Critical vs. Non-Critical Violations

Under Utah's R392-100 Food Service Sanitation Rule (adopted from the FDA Food Code 2013), violations are classified into two main risk tiers, both of which the underlying data model consolidates into `violation_codes.critical` (true/false):

**Priority Item ("Critical 1")** — a provision "whose application contributes directly to the elimination, prevention, or reduction to an acceptable level of hazards associated with foodborne illness or injury, and there is no provision that more directly controls the hazard." These are typically quantifiable control points like proper cooking/reheating/cooling temperatures and handwashing. In the Code they're marked with a superscript **P**. Utah's rule also lumps in "Priority Foundation Item" (Critical 2) violations — provisions that *support or enable* a Priority Item (e.g., staff training, HACCP documentation, labeling), marked **Pf**. In this dataset both are simply flagged `critical = true`.

**Core Item ("non-critical")** — "a provision in the Model Code that is not designated as a Priority Item or Priority Foundation Item," usually relating to general sanitation, facility upkeep, or operational controls rather than a direct, quantifiable illness-hazard control.

**Correction timing differs too**: the regulation requires a Priority Item violation be corrected *at the time of inspection* (or within 72 hours if the inspector allows), a Priority Foundation Item within 10 calendar days, while Core/non-critical items get more routine handling.

### Effect on inspection score
Pulling actual inspection data (22,339 non-critical vs. 9,690 critical violation citations logged):

- **Critical violations drive the score up much faster.** Inspections with 0 critical violations average a score of **3.1**; that climbs to **8.5** with 1 critical violation, **14.7** with 2, **21.4** with 3, **28.5** with 4, and **34.8** with 5 — roughly +6-7 points per additional critical violation.
- **Non-critical violations add points too, but much more slowly.** Going from 0 to 1 non-critical violation barely moves the average score (9.2 → 5.6, reflecting that inspections with zero non-critical items often still have critical ones), and the climb per additional non-critical violation is only about +2 points (e.g., 5 → 14.7, 10 → 26.5), compared to the +6-7 per critical violation.

In short: since a higher inspection score = worse (0 is clean), **critical violations are weighted far more heavily** because they represent direct, immediate foodborne-illness hazards, while non-critical (Core) violations reflect general sanitation/operational issues and add to the score at a noticeably slower rate. The charts above show this side-by-side (score vs. # critical, score vs. # non-critical), plus the overall citation mix (~30% of all cited violations are critical, ~70% non-critical).
