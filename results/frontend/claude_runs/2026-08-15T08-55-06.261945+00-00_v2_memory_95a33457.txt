## Finding halal food in Salt Lake County: not too hard, but options are concentrated and inspection data is thin

**How many halal options exist**

Looking at the "Halal food" service attribute Google tags on menus/listings, **70 of the 3,595 restaurants** located within Salt Lake County's bounding box (~1.9%) explicitly offer halal food. Only **one** listing in the whole dataset is categorized specifically as a "Halal restaurant" (Midnimo Restaurant) — the rest are halal-friendly Indian, Afghan, Somali, Mediterranean/Middle Eastern, and Nepali kitchens that flag halal as an attribute rather than being halal-exclusive.

Highly-rated, well-reviewed examples turned up by search include:
- **Global Place Somali Restaurant** (4.9★, 418 reviews, Salt Lake City)
- **O'Falafel Middle Eastern Cuisine** (4.6★, 968 reviews, Salt Lake City)
- **Bombay House** (4.7★, 2,093 reviews, Salt Lake City)
- **Shahrazad Market and Restaurant** (4.3★, 764 reviews, Salt Lake City)
- **Saffron Valley – South Jordan** (4.6★, 1,068 reviews)

**Geographic spread**

Halal-tagged spots are concentrated in **Salt Lake City proper (32 of the 70)**, with much smaller clusters in Sandy (8), West Valley City (6), West Jordan (4), and Holladay (4). Southwest-side suburbs like Riverton, Millcreek, and South Salt Lake have only 1–2 each — so halal options thin out noticeably the farther you get from the urban core, meaning residents in outer suburbs have to travel toward SLC/Sandy/West Valley for reliable choices.

**Quality**

The rating histogram shows halal-tagged establishments skew high — most cluster in the 4.3–4.9★ range, so the ones that exist tend to be well-regarded, not just a few scattered mediocre options.

**Inspection transparency gap**

Of these 70 halal-tagged establishments, only **1** has a matched SLCHD inspection record in this dataset — consistent with the overall pattern that ~98.4% of establishments in the database lack matched inspection history. So while halal food is findable and generally well-rated by diners, there's almost no public health-inspection paper trail to independently verify food-safety compliance at these specific places; that has to be checked directly via SLCHD's portal rather than through this dataset.

**Bottom line:** Halal food is a small but real niche (~2% of county restaurants), concentrated heavily in Salt Lake City with good customer ratings, but "hard to verify" from an inspection-transparency standpoint since almost none of the halal-tagged spots have matched inspection records here.
