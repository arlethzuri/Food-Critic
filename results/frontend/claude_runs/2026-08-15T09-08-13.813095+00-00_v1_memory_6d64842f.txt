## Critical vs. Non-Critical Violations

**Definition (from `violation_codes.critical`):** Every violation code in the SLCHD dataset is permanently flagged as either **critical** or **non-critical** — this is a fixed property of the code itself (no code has ever been cited both ways). Critical violations are the more serious food-safety issues (e.g. temperature control, cross-contamination, sanitation practices) that pose direct health risk, while non-critical violations tend to be lower-risk/procedural items (e.g. facility maintenance, documentation). Across the dataset there are 98 distinct critical codes (9,690 citations total) and 139 distinct non-critical codes (22,339 citations total).

There's also a secondary, unverified signal: `asterisk_count` (0/1/2 trailing `*` on the raw code) correlates with criticality but its official meaning per the Utah Food Code wasn't independently confirmed — treat it as an observed pattern, not a rule.

**Effect on inspection score** (`inspection_score`: 0 = clean, higher = worse):

- **Clean inspections** (no violations at all): avg score **1.7** (1,108 inspections).
- **Non-critical violations only**: avg score **3.07** (2,073 inspections).
- **Critical violations only**: avg score **9.18** (530 inspections) — much higher than non-critical-only despite often fewer violations (avg 1.64 critical vs 2.78 non-critical per inspection in their respective groups).
- **Mixed** (both types present): avg score **15.67** (4,309 inspections, the most common case).

Looking at the marginal effect of each additional violation:
- Each additional **critical** violation adds roughly **+6 points** on average (0→2.66, 1→8.5, 2→14.72, 3→21.38, 4→28.48, 5→34.77, 6→43.33).
- Each additional **non-critical** violation adds only roughly **+2 points** on average (0→4.5, 1→5.6, 2→7.83, 3→9.88, 4→12.03, ... 8→26.3).

So critical violations weigh roughly **3x as heavily** on the score per occurrence as non-critical ones, which is exactly what you'd expect if the score is meant to reflect actual food-safety risk. The three charts above show: (1) average score by violation profile (clean vs. non-critical-only vs. critical-only vs. mixed), (2) the steep score increase per additional critical violation, and (3) the more gradual increase per additional non-critical violation.
