## Correlation between ratings and violations

Across the **127 restaurants** in the dataset that have a matched SLCHD inspection history (the only ones we can check against violations — the other 98.4% of Google-listed establishments have no matched inspection record at all), I computed Pearson correlations between Google `avg_rating` and violation counts:

- **avg_rating vs. total violation count: r = ‑0.17** (weak negative)
- **avg_rating vs. critical violation count: r = ‑0.02** (essentially no relationship)

**Interpretation:** There is no meaningful correlation. Google ratings do not track food-safety violation history in this data. If anything, total violations show only a slight negative trend, but it's too weak (r≈‑0.17) to be practically meaningful, and critical violations (the more serious kind) show basically zero relationship (r≈‑0.02).

This is visible in the scatter plot: many highly-rated restaurants (4.4–4.7 stars) — e.g. **Sagato Bakery & Cafe** (4.5★, 47 critical / 88 total violations), **Stella Grill** (4.5★, 38 critical / 123 total), **Siegfried's Delicatessen** (4.7★, 34 critical / 64 total), and **Navajo Hogan** (4.7★, 33 critical / 83 total) — actually carry some of the *highest* violation counts in the whole dataset, while lower-rated chains like Burger King (3.2★) or Little Caesars (3.6★) have fewer violations by comparison. This confirms that customers' star ratings reflect taste, service, and experience — not the health-inspection record, which they generally can't see.

**Caveat:** This analysis only covers the 127 establishments with matched SLCHD inspection data (out of thousands of Google listings), so it should be read as suggestive rather than a county-wide conclusion.
