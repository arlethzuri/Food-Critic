## Correlation between ratings and violations

**Important caveat first:** only 127 establishments in the dataset have both a Google rating *and* a matched SLCHD inspection history (98.4% of establishments overall have no matched inspection record at all, per the tool's own note) — so this analysis covers just that small matched subset, not the full restaurant population.

**Correlation coefficients (n=127):**
- Rating vs. total violations: **r ≈ -0.17** (weak negative)
- Rating vs. critical violations: **r ≈ -0.02** (essentially no relationship)

So there's only a very weak tendency for higher-rated places to have somewhat fewer *total* violations, and virtually no relationship at all when you isolate *critical* (public-health-significant) violations. A Google star rating tells you almost nothing about whether a restaurant has serious food-safety citations.

**Breaking it down by rating bucket confirms this:**
| Rating bucket | Avg total violations | Avg critical violations | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| <3.5 | 53.8 | 14.2 | 5 |
| 3.5–3.99 | 43.6 | 11.1 | 31 |
| 4.0–4.49 | 42.4 | 11.4 | 48 |
| 4.5+ | 39.0 | 12.3 | 43 |

There's a mild downward drift in total violations as rating rises (53.8 → 39.0), but it's noisy and driven by a very small <3.5 bucket (only 5 establishments). Critical violations actually tick back *up* slightly at the top rating bucket (4.5+ averages 12.3 critical violations vs. 11.1–11.4 in the middle bands), reinforcing that a high star rating is not a reliable signal of food-safety compliance — reviewers are rating food/service/ambiance, not sanitation inspection outcomes.

Charts provided:
1. **Scatter plot** of rating vs. total violations across all 127 matched establishments — shows a wide, noisy spread with no tight trend line.
2. **Bar chart** of average total violations by rating bucket — shows the modest downward drift described above.
