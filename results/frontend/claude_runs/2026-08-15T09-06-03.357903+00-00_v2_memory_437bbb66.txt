## What an inspection score means

The `inspection_score` in the Salt Lake County Health Department (SLCHD) data works like a **demerit/penalty score, not a grade** — the tool description confirms **0 = clean, and higher numbers = worse** (more/more serious violations found). It is *not* a percentage or letter grade out of 100 in the traditional sense; it accumulates points based on violations found during a visit.

### How violations drive the score (per Utah's R392-100 Food Service Sanitation Rule)
Violations cited during an inspection fall into three tiers, and the score reflects how serious and how many were found:

1. **Priority Item ("Critical 1")** — a provision whose direct application "contributes directly to the elimination, prevention, or reduction to an acceptable level" of foodborne-illness hazards (e.g., cooking/cooling/reheating temperatures, handwashing). These carry the most weight and, per §8-405.11, generally must be corrected **at the time of inspection** (or within 72 hours).
2. **Priority Foundation Item ("Critical 2")** — supports/enables priority items (e.g., staff training, HACCP documentation, labeling). Correction is allowed up to **10 calendar days**.
3. **Core Item ("non-critical")** — general sanitation/operational items not tied to an immediate illness risk; these still add points but are lower severity.

The regulation also ties inspection **frequency** to past scores/performance: establishments are inspected at least every 6 months, but risk-based scheduling (§8-401.20) means those with a history of priority/priority-foundation violations or repeat core-item violations get inspected **more often**.

In this dataset, `critical` violations (flagged in `violation_codes.critical`) are what `search_establishments`' `critical_violation_count` and `min_critical_violations` filters track — i.e., the Priority/Priority-Foundation items above, which are the biggest drivers of a high inspection score.

### The actual score distribution in this data
The chart above shows the distribution of `inspection_score` across all recorded SLCHD inspections: most inspections cluster at **low scores near 0**, with a long right tail of inspections scoring much higher, meaning most visits are found broadly compliant while a smaller number of inspections rack up substantially more violation points. Keep in mind only **1.6% of establishments in this database have any matched SLCHD inspection history at all** — for the rest, we simply have no inspection score, not evidence of a clean record.
