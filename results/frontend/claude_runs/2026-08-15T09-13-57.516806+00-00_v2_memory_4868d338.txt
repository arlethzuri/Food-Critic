## What "cold holding" means

"Cold holding" refers to keeping **potentially hazardous (Time/Temperature Control for Safety) food at or below 41°F (5°C)** any time it isn't actively being prepared, cooked, or cooled. This is governed by Utah's R392-100 Food Service Sanitation Rule (based on FDA Food Code 2013), notably:

- **§3-501.16 (Time/Temperature Control for Safety Food, Hot and Cold Holding)** — the core rule requiring cold TCS food to stay ≤41°F except during prep/cooking/cooling.
- **§3-202.11 (Temperature at Receiving)** — refrigerated TCS food must arrive at ≤41°F.
- **§3-501.19 (Time as a Public Health Control)** — an alternative to temperature control, but food must start at ≤41°F when time-tracking begins.

In the inspection data, "cold holding" as an issue category actually spans several distinct violation codes cited by SLC Health Department inspectors, ranging from the equipment/support issues to the critical temperature failure itself:

| Code | Critical? | Times cited | What it covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.4.85 | No | 2,176 | Cold holding equipment (inside/outside) unclean |
| 4.6.41 | No | 1,975 | Floor dirty beneath cold holding equipment |
| 4.4.63 | No | 1,661 | Cold holding unit malfunction (e.g., condenser leaking, ice buildup) |
| **4.3.59** | **Yes** | **1,022** | **Food actually held out of temperature** (e.g., "shredded cheese held at 56°F on a counter cold holding unit") — this is the direct violation of the 41°F rule |
| 4.4.37 | No/Yes (mixed) | 48 | Cold holding unit lacks a thermometer to verify temperature |
| 4.3.57 | Yes | 35 | Related cold-holding temperature control failure |

The **critical** version (4.3.59, cited 1,022 times) is the one that matters most for public health — it means food was actually measured out of the safe temperature range, which allows bacterial growth and is a direct foodborne-illness risk. The non-critical codes (dirty equipment, no thermometer, malfunctioning unit) are supporting/preventive issues that can lead to an actual temperature failure if uncorrected.

The chart above shows how often each of these cold-holding-related codes has been cited across Salt Lake County inspections in the dataset — equipment cleanliness/floor issues are the most frequently cited, but the critical "food held out of temperature" violation (4.3.59) is still cited over 1,000 times, making it one of the more common critical violations countywide.
