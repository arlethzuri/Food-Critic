## Cold holding

**Cold holding** means keeping refrigerated **time/temperature control for safety (TCS) food** cold while it is stored, displayed, or served—for example, in a refrigerator, prep cooler, salad bar, or cold display.

Under Utah’s food-service rule, TCS food must generally be held at **41°F (5°C) or below** when it is not being prepared, cooked, cooled, or managed under an approved time-as-a-public-health-control procedure. A “cold holding” issue therefore means an inspector found—or documented concern that—food requiring refrigeration was not being maintained cold enough.

### Why it matters
When refrigerated foods warm above the required holding temperature, bacteria can grow more readily. The concern commonly applies to foods such as cooked meats, dairy products, cut produce, prepared salads, and other perishable ready-to-eat foods.

### Important nuance
A cold-holding finding does **not** by itself establish that someone became ill or that food was necessarily discarded. The significance depends on the measured temperature, how long the food was out of temperature control, the food involved, and the corrective action taken.

**Regulatory basis:** Utah R392-100 / FDA Food Code 2013 §3-501.16(A)(2): TCS food must be maintained at **5°C (41°F) or less** for cold holding.
