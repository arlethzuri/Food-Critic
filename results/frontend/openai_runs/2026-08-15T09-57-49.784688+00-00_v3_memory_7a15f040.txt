## Critical vs. non-critical violations

| Category | What it means | Correction expectation | Effect on inspection score |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | In Utah’s Food Code terminology, these are generally **Priority** or **Priority Foundation** items—conditions tied more directly to foodborne-illness risk or to the controls that prevent it. Priority Foundation items are also called “critical 2” and include management systems such as training, equipment, HACCP documentation, and labeling. | The rule calls for correction **at the inspection** when possible. If more time is allowed, a **Priority** item may receive up to **72 hours** and a **Priority Foundation** item or HACCP deviation up to **10 calendar days**. | A critical citation contributes to the inspection’s recorded score and is treated as more consequential than a core/non-critical issue in the inspection framework. The sources retrieved do **not** provide a numeric point schedule, so I can’t responsibly state a fixed number of points per violation. |
| **Non-critical** | Utah defines a **Core Item** as “non critical”: a provision not designated Priority or Priority Foundation. These usually concern general sanitation, operational controls, and sanitation standard operating procedures. | They still require correction on the timeframe specified by the regulatory authority, but they are not handled under the Priority/Priority Foundation correction deadlines above. | A non-critical citation can also raise the recorded inspection score, but it represents a lower immediate food-safety priority than a critical item. The retrieved rule text does not specify a universal numeric point value. |

### How to interpret the score
- In the local inspection data, **0 is clean and higher scores are worse**; therefore, cited violations—critical or non-critical—move the score in the unfavorable direction.
- The key distinction is **risk and urgency**, not merely whether an item appears on the report: critical/Priority items receive faster correction expectations, while core/non-critical items generally reflect foundational sanitation and operational conditions.
- Repeated core violations can still matter: Utah’s risk-based inspection provisions say inspection frequency may be increased for **numerous or repeat Core Item violations**, as well as for Priority/Priority Foundation nonconformance.
