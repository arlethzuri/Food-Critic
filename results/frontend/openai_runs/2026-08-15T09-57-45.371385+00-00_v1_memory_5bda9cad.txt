--- Answer ---
A **critical violation** is a citation whose violation code is flagged as `critical` in the SLCHD portal data. A **non-critical violation** is one flagged `critical = false`. The dataset does not provide a further narrative definition of the risk distinction or a published point schedule by violation type, so I can’t responsibly claim a fixed number of score points per critical versus non-critical citation.

For scoring:

- The **inspection score is total violation points** for that visit: **0 means clean, and higher scores are worse**.
- Both critical and non-critical citations can contribute to the score, but the available data do **not** establish a universal “critical = X points / non-critical = Y points” rule.
- In the recorded inspections, critical citations appeared in **9,690 cited-violation rows** (**10,744 occurrences**), while non-critical citations appeared in **22,339 rows** (**25,862 occurrences**).
- Inspections containing a critical citation had an average score of **21.6** (among citation rows), compared with **15.9** for inspections containing a non-critical citation. That association does not by itself prove the point value of either type, since inspections can contain multiple citations of both types.

The chart shows average inspection scores grouped by whether an inspection had critical citations, non-critical citations, both, or no cited violations.
