## What an inspection score represents

An **inspection score** is the numeric result recorded for a specific Salt Lake County Health Department food-establishment inspection. In this dataset, it is stored as an integer on each inspection visit; it is **not** a restaurant rating and should not be compared directly with Google’s 1–5 customer rating.

### How to interpret it
- **Lower is better:** a score of **0** corresponds to no cited violations on that inspection in this dataset; higher scores indicate more or/or more consequential cited inspection issues.
- It is a **snapshot of one visit**, not a permanent grade or a prediction of current conditions.
- Look at the **inspection date, inspection type, cited violations, and repeat history** alongside the score. A single score alone does not say which food-safety practice was involved.
- An inspection with **no violation rows is treated as clean in this data**, rather than as missing violation detail.

### Important context
Utah’s food-service rule requires the regulatory authority to inspect food establishments at least every **six months** in the ordinary case, with risk-based exceptions; the rule also distinguishes how violations are handled during inspection and correction. Salt Lake County’s inspection-process page directs the public to its food-service inspection scores and rankings. [R392-100, §8-401.10; Salt Lake County Health Department — Food Inspection Process, p.1]

### Data caveat
Only **139 of 8,592 establishments (1.6%)** in this dataset have a matched Salt Lake County inspection listing. For the other **98.4%**, absence of inspection history here does **not** mean the restaurant had a clean inspection—it means there is no matched record in this dataset.

### Visualization
The chart shows the distribution of recorded inspection-score values across inspection visits. Because scores are recorded per visit, use it as context—not as a substitute for reviewing the actual violations and dates for a particular establishment.
