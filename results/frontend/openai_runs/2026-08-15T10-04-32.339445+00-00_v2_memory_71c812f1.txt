--- Answer ---
**Cold holding** means keeping refrigerated, temperature-controlled foods cold enough to limit bacterial growth while they are stored or displayed for service.

Under Utah’s food-service rule, **time/temperature control for safety (TCS) foods** must generally be held at **41°F (5°C) or below** when cold-held. Examples include cooked meats, dairy products, cut melons, cooked rice or pasta, and prepared foods that require refrigeration.

A “cold holding” issue typically means an inspector found food being kept **above the required cold temperature**—for example, in a cooler, prep table, salad bar, or ice bath that was not maintaining safe temperatures. This matters because warmer temperatures can allow harmful bacteria to multiply.

The rule also requires cold-holding equipment to have temperature-measuring devices positioned to reflect the warmest area of refrigerated storage.
