--- Answer ---
An **inspection score** is a summary measure from a food-safety inspection. In this dataset, **0 is clean and higher scores are worse**—they indicate more or more serious issues documented at the establishment’s **most recent** inspection.

The score should be read as a snapshot, not a permanent grade:

- It reflects conditions observed on that inspection date.
- A higher score does **not** by itself establish that someone became ill; it signals more documented compliance concerns requiring correction.
- Inspectors focus especially on **priority** and **priority-foundation** items, which are more directly tied to foodborne-illness prevention, as well as repeated or numerous **core** violations.
- Utah’s rule requires routine inspections at least every six months in general, while allowing inspection frequency to be adjusted using a risk-based assessment of compliance history and foodborne-illness potential. citeR392-100 Food Service Sanitation Rule, §§8-401.10 & 8-401.20

The chart summarizes the three major factors the rule identifies for inspection attention: priority-related violations, core violations, and the establishment’s compliance/risk history.
