There is a **weak negative correlation** between Google ratings and cited violations among the **139 restaurants with both a matched inspection history and a rating**:

- **Rating vs. total cited violation rows:** Pearson correlation **−0.246**
- **Rating vs. critical-violation rows:** Pearson correlation **−0.101**

In practical terms, restaurants with higher ratings tend to have *somewhat* fewer total violations, but the relationship is weak and not reliable enough to use ratings as a food-safety proxy. The relationship with critical violations is especially minimal.

For context, this matched group averaged **4.24 stars**, **38.35 total cited violation rows**, and **10.75 critical-violation rows** per restaurant across its available inspection history.

The scatterplot shows substantial spread at nearly every rating level—restaurants with similar customer ratings can have very different violation histories. Also, most establishments (**98.4%**) have no matched SLCHD inspection history, so this analysis applies only to the relatively small matched subset.
