A **critical violation** is a condition more directly tied to foodborne-illness risk—typically a **Priority** or **Priority Foundation** item under Utah’s food code. Examples commonly involve controls such as safe food temperatures, preventing contamination, employee hygiene, or required food-safety procedures.

A **non-critical violation** generally corresponds to a **Core** item: an issue that is still a code violation but is less directly connected to an immediate food-safety hazard. Core items often concern facility upkeep, equipment condition, cleaning, or operational practices.

### Effect on inspection scores
- **Critical / priority-related violations:** carry the greater score impact. They are treated as more serious because they relate more directly to controlling foodborne-illness hazards.
- **Non-critical / core violations:** have a smaller score impact individually, but repeated or numerous core violations can still matter. Utah’s rule specifically says inspection frequency may be increased for establishments with numerous or repeat Core-item violations.

In both cases, **a higher inspection score means more violations/greater concern; a score of 0 is clean** in this dataset’s inspection-score field. The exact point value for an individual cited item was not provided in the retrieved regulatory material, so I can’t responsibly assign a numeric per-violation penalty.

Utah’s rule requires inspectors to document violations and specify correction time frames. It distinguishes correction requirements for Priority/Priority Foundation items from those for Core items. citeR392-100 Food Service Sanitation Rule, §§8-403.10–8-406
