### What Does "Cold Holding" Mean?

In food safety regulation and health inspections, **Cold Holding** refers to the requirement that perishable **Time/Temperature Control for Safety (TCS)** foods—such as meats, dairy, cut tomatoes, cooked pasta, cheeses, cut leafy greens, and salsas—must be held cold at **41°F (5°C) or below**.

---

### Key Concepts & Regulatory Meaning

1. **The Temperature Danger Zone (41°F to 135°F):**
   * Pathogenic bacteria (such as *Listeria monocytogenes*, *Salmonella*, *E. coli*, and *Staphylococcus aureus*) multiply rapidly when food temperatures rise above 41°F.
   * Proper cold holding keeps food outside this danger zone to prevent bacterial proliferation and foodborne illness.

2. **Critical Violation Status:**
   * Under Salt Lake County Health Department (SLCHD) regulations and the Utah Health Code (FDA Food Code standards), **Cold Holding** (Code `4.3.59`) is classified as a **Critical Violation**. 
   * Critical violations are directly linked to an increased risk of foodborne illness and typically require immediate corrective action on site (e.g., rapid chilling, moving food to a working unit, or discarding product held out of temperature too long).

---

### Cold Holding in Salt Lake County Inspection Data

Analysis of the Salt Lake County inspection records reveals the following key metrics:

* **Prevalence:** Cold holding violations have been cited **727 times** across **714 health inspections**.
* **Rank among Critical Violations:** It is the **3rd most frequent critical violation** overall in Salt Lake County (behind equipment contact surface sanitation and staff eating/drinking in prep areas).
* **Primary Temperature Hazard:** It is the **#1 temperature-related violation**, cited nearly **2.5 times as often as Hot Holding** (296 citations).
