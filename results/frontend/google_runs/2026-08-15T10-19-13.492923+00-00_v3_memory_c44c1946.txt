--- Answer ---
In Utah food safety regulations (Utah Admin Code R392-100 / FDA Food Code 2013 § 3-501.16) and Salt Lake County health inspections, the issue category Cold Holding refers to the requirement that cold Time/Temperature Control for Safety (TCS) foods—such as meats, dairy, cut leafy greens, cut tomatoes, and cooked dishes—must be maintained at an internal temperature of 41°F (5°C) or lower during storage, display, and service.

Key details regarding Cold Holding include:
1. Public Health Rationale: The range between 41°F and 135°F is known as the 'temperature danger zone.' Maintaining TCS foods at 41°F or lower prevents or drastically slows the growth of foodborne pathogens such as Listeria monocytogenes, Salmonella, E. coli, and Staphylococcus aureus.
2. Violation Severity: In Salt Lake County health inspections, Cold Holding (Violation Code 4.3.59) is classified as a Critical Violation (Priority Item) because temperature abuse directly increases the risk of foodborne illness.
3. Frequency in Salt Lake County: Inspection records show that Cold Holding is the 3rd most cited critical violation across Salt Lake County establishments, with 727 total citations recorded.
