In Salt Lake County health inspections, an **inspection score** represents the total cumulative violation points assigned to an establishment during a health inspection visit.

### Key Principles of Inspection Scoring:
1. **Lower is Better (0 is Clean):** Unlike customer rating systems where higher numbers are desirable, health inspection scores measure non-compliance. A score of **0** indicates a completely clean inspection with zero cited violations.
2. **Violation Point Accumulation:** Points are assessed based on health code violations observed by Salt Lake County Health Department (SLCHD) inspectors. Critical violations (such as improper temperature control or cross-contamination risk) contribute more heavily to elevated inspection scores compared to non-critical core item violations.
3. **Score Interpretation in Salt Lake County Data (7,798 Scored Inspections):**
   - **0 (Clean):** 710 inspections (9.1%) had no violation points.
   - **1–10 (Low Severity):** 4,301 inspections (55.2%) had minor infractions, with an average score of 5.04.
   - **11–25 (Moderate Severity):** 2,183 inspections (28.0%) had an average score of 16.04.
   - **26–50 (Elevated Risk):** 531 inspections (6.8%) had significant compliance issues, with an average score of 33.07.
   - **51+ (High Severity/Risk):** 73 inspections (0.9%) exhibited severe or systemic failures, averaging 82.11 points (peak score recorded: 297).

Across all routine inspections in the dataset (7,543 visits), the overall average inspection score is **10.45** with a median score of **8**.
