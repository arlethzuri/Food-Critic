### Executive Summary

Across Salt Lake County restaurants, there is **virtually no correlation** between a restaurant's **Google customer rating** and its **health department inspection performance** (critical violations or overall inspection score). 

While there is a slight negative correlation with total violation counts per inspection ($r = -0.230$), critical health violations—the violations that pose direct risk to food safety—show almost zero correlation with consumer ratings ($r = -0.066$).

---

### Key Data & Correlation Metrics

The statistical analysis across the 139 Salt Lake County establishments with matched SLCHD inspection history reveals the following Pearson correlation coefficients ($r$):

| Relationship Metric | Correlation Coefficient ($r$) | Interpretation |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Google Rating vs. Inspection Score** | **$-0.041$** | **No correlation** |
| **Google Rating vs. Critical Violations / Inspection** | **$-0.066$** | **No correlation** |
| **Google Rating vs. Total Violations / Inspection** | **$-0.230$** | **Weak negative correlation** |
| **Google Rating vs. Total Lifetime Violations** | **$-0.246$** | **Weak negative correlation** |
| **Google Rating vs. Number of Inspections** | **$-0.315$** | **Weak negative correlation** |

| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |

#### Observations:
1. **Critical Violations are Identical Across Rating Tiers:** Whether a restaurant is rated 3.5–3.9 stars, 4.0–4.4 stars, or 4.5–5.0 stars, it averages approximately **1.21 to 1.24 critical violations per inspection**. High customer ratings do not shield a restaurant from critical safety citations.
2. **Lowest Rated Tier (< 3.5 Stars):** Restaurants in the lowest star tier exhibit higher total violations (6.10/inspection) and higher average inspection scores (22.61), but represent a small subset (5 restaurants).
3. **High Ratings Do Not Mean Clean Inspections:** Top-rated restaurants (4.5–5.0 stars) actually average a slightly higher inspection score (14.68) than mid-tier restaurants (13.52 in the 4.0–4.4 range), demonstrating that consumer praise is uncorrelated with health inspection severity.

---

### Regulatory Context & Why Customer Ratings Diverge

Under Utah’s **R392-100 Food Service Sanitation Rule** (adopting the FDA Food Code):
* **Inspection Focus:** Environmental health inspectors evaluate back-of-house compliance, such as cold/hot food holding temperatures, cross-contamination hazards, dishwashing chemical concentrations, handwashing sink accessibility, and date marking for perishable foods.
* **Consumer Perspective:** Google ratings reflect front-of-house customer experiences—flavor, portion size, pricing, order speed, staff friendliness, and seating atmosphere.
* **Visibility Gap:** Because customers rarely observe food preparation procedures, chemical storage, or walk-in cooler temperatures, a restaurant can receive 5-star customer reviews while simultaneously receiving critical health citations.
