--- Answer ---
In Salt Lake County and under Utah’s **R392-100 Food Service Sanitation Rule** (adapted from the FDA Food Code), an **inspection score** represents the **total accumulated demerit points** assigned during an inspection visit.

---

### Key Rules of the Scoring System

1. **Lower is Better (0 = Perfectly Clean)**
   * **Score = 0**: Represents a clean inspection with zero demerits cited.
   * **Higher Score = Worse Outcome**: Unlike academic grading where high percentages are good, health inspection scores measure accrued penalty/demerit points. Higher scores indicate more violations or greater health risks.

2. **Violation Severity Weighting**
   * **Non-Critical (Core) Items**: Relate to general facility cleanliness, equipment maintenance, or operational standards. Non-critical violations typically carry low demerits (**1 to 2 points** each, averaging **1.08 points** in single-violation inspections).
   * **Critical (Priority / Priority Foundation) Items**: Relate directly to foodborne illness risk factors (e.g., inadequate hot/cold holding temperatures, lack of handwashing, cross-contamination, improper cooking). Critical violations carry higher penalty points (**3, 6, or 12 points** each, averaging **5.05 points** in single-violation inspections).

---

### Salt Lake County Inspection Score Distribution

Across **8,020 recorded inspections** in Salt Lake County:

* **0 (Clean)**: **710** inspections (8.9%)
* **1 – 5 (Low Demerits)**: **2,363** inspections (29.5%)
* **6 – 15 (Moderate Demerits)**: **3,066** inspections (38.2%)
* **16 – 30 (High Demerits)**: **1,278** inspections (15.9%)
* **31+ (Very High Demerits)**: **603** inspections (7.5%)

#### Inspection Scores by Visit Type
* **Routine Inspections**: Average **10.4 points** (median **8**).
* **Follow-up Inspections**: Average **6.2 points** (median **3**), reflecting corrected violations after an initial citation.
* **Critical Item Inspections**: Average **9.0 points** (median **3**).
