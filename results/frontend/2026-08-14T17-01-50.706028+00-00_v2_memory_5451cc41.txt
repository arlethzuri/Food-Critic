--- Answer ---
In Salt Lake County, an inspection score is a numerical value used to communicate the results of a health inspection. While the specific mathematical formula can vary by jurisdiction, the key takeaway for consumers is:

**A lower score is better.** 

*   **0 (or near zero):** Indicates a "clean" inspection with no significant violations found.
*   **Higher scores:** Indicate a higher number or more severe types of violations.

The scoring system is based on the severity of the findings, which are categorized into different levels:

1.  **Priority Items:** These are the most critical violations that pose the highest risk to public health (e.g., improper food temperature).
2.  **Priority Foundation Items:** These are items that support food safety but are slightly less immediate in risk than priority items (e.g., issues with handwashing stations).
3.  **Core Items:** These are fundamental food safety practices (e.g., cleanliness of surfaces) that, while important, do not pose an immediate health hazard.

When a restaurant receives a high score, it means the inspector found more of these violations during their visit. Most establishments in our database (98.4%) do not have a recorded inspection history in this system, so when you see a score, it is specifically tied to the most recent official inspection conducted by the health department.
