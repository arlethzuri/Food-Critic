--- Answer ---
In the context of health inspections in Salt Lake County, **"cold holding"** refers to the practice of maintaining potentially hazardous foods (such as milk, cheese, or meat) at safe, refrigerated temperatures—typically **at or below 41°F**—to prevent the growth of harmful bacteria.

Based on inspection records, "cold holding" issues generally fall into three main categories:

*   **Temperature Violations:** Food being held at unsafe temperatures. For example, inspections have cited milk being held at 47°F or 58°F instead of the required temperature.
*   **Equipment & Maintenance Issues:** Refrigeration units that are not operating properly, lack necessary electronic monitoring for time and temperature, or do not have sufficient capacity to hold food safely.
*   **Knowledge & Documentation Gaps:** Staff being unable to demonstrate adequate knowledge of proper cold holding temperatures, or a failure to maintain required cooling and cold holding records (often seen in specialized processes like Reduced Oxygen Packaging).

When these standards are not met, it is often classified as a critical violation because improper temperature control is a primary cause of foodborne illness.
