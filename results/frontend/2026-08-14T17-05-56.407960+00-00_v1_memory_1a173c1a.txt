--- Answer ---
The distinction between critical and non-critical violations is based on the level of risk they pose to public health:

*   **Critical Violations:** These are high-risk issues that present an immediate threat to food safety (e.g., improper holding temperatures, cross-contamination, or poor hand hygiene).
*   **Non-Critical Violations:** These are lower-risk issues that do not pose an immediate health hazard but still violate sanitary standards (e.g., minor maintenance needs or labeling errors).

### How They Affect the Inspection Score
The **inspection score** is a cumulative point total where **0 represents a perfect, clean inspection**. A higher score indicates more significant violations and a greater risk to public health. 

While both types of violations contribute to the final score, critical violations are typically weighted much more heavily because of their direct impact on foodborne illness risks. As shown in the chart below, as the total number of violations increases, the average inspection score rises (indicating a worse inspection).
